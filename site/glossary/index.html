GEO and AEO glossary: AI search terms explained

Generative engine optimization (GEO)

Work that helps a brand’s pages get cited, and its name mentioned, in answers written by generative AI, such as Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini.

Answer engine optimization (AEO)

Optimizing content so answer engines (AI assistants and AI search) use it to answer a question. Close to GEO; the two names are often used for the same work.

LLM SEO

Another name for GEO: search optimization aimed at large language models (LLMs) and the AI search tools built on them.

AI search visibility

How often a brand or website appears in AI answers for the questions that matter to it, as a cited source or a named brand.

Google AI Overview

The AI-written summary Google shows at the top of some search results, with links to the sources it used. Not every search shows one.

Google AI Mode

Google’s conversational AI search, where the whole result is an AI answer with cited sources and follow-up questions.

Answer engine

A tool that replies with a written answer instead of a list of links, such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot or Google AI Mode.

AI citation

A link to a web page that an AI answer shows as one of its sources. A citation can send visitors, so it is the main thing GEO measures.

Brand mention (named, not cited)

An AI answer that says a brand’s name in its text without linking to the brand’s site. It builds awareness but sends no visit.

Citation rate

The share of checked questions whose AI answer cites your site. CiteFox calls this the score.

AI share of voice

How often each brand or site is cited across a set of AI answers, compared with the others. It shows who the engines prefer for your topic.

Buyer prompt

A question a real buyer would type into AI search, often long and situational, such as “best invoicing tool for a five-person agency that bills hourly”.

Ideal customer profile (ICP)

A short description of who buys from you and what sends them looking. CiteFox writes buyer prompts from it.

Target page

The page of your site that should be cited for a question. If an answer cites a different page of yours, that page is competing with your target.

Query fan-out

A technique Google describes for AI Mode: one question is split into several related searches, and the answer draws on the results of all of them.

Zero-click search

A search where the person gets an answer on the results page, from an AI Overview or another feature, and does not click through to a website.

Market

A country and language pair, such as United States · English. AI answers and their sources differ by market, so each is checked on its own.

Fix brief

A short note of what to change on a page so it answers a set of buyer questions better, ready to hand to a writer.

GEO vs SEO

SEO works to rank a page in the list of search results. GEO (generative engine optimization) and AEO (answer engine optimization) work to get a page cited as a source, or a brand named, inside an AI-written answer.

AI answers appear above the links or replace them. A page can rank well and still not be cited, and AI engines also cite pages that do not rank at the top.

Each engine picks its own sources, and answers change by country, language and over time. So citations have to be measured per engine and market, and checked again after every change.
